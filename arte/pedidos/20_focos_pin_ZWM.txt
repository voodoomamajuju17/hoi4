style: macro product photograph of ONE real, physical collectible pin badge, photorealistic, made of true materials (see MATERIAL), the way a real enamel pin or military badge looks on a jeweller's photo: real metal reflections, real enamel gloss, tiny honest wear on the edges. Shot straight-on from the front, soft studio light from the top left, everything in sharp focus. The badge carries ONE simple, bold emblem (at most two or three elements) that stands for the focus; its outline is one clear die-cut shape (round, shield, hexagon, gear, or the silhouette of the emblem itself). Big shapes and strong contrast between metal and enamel: it must read clearly at 95x85 pixels, nothing tiny or fussy. It is a PIN, not a coin or a medal: the outline follows the emblem (a die-cut silhouette) and is a plain round disc only when the emblem itself is round; no border ring full of extra symbols, no second emblem, no background pattern. Badge centred, filling about 85% of the canvas, same camera, light and scale for the whole set. Isolated on a fully transparent background: no table, no backing card, no pin clasp visible, no cast shadow beyond a faint contact shadow. No text, no letters, no numbers, no watermark.
material: an improvised badge hand-cut from scrap tin and rusted steel, hand-painted chipped enamel in two colours, visible rivets and file marks, made by survivors
