REGLAS COMUNES (pegarlas junto con cada bloque)
====================================================================

what this image is:
- A real documentary photograph, taken by a person with a real camera, of a real PRINTED object (magazine, poster, newspaper, notice) that exists in a real place in the year 2100. The printed object was designed by a human graphic designer of that world, printed on a real press, and then handled, carried, pasted, rained on, folded. We see the object AND a slice of the place around it.
- It is NOT a flat digital mock-up, NOT a vector poster, NOT a "poster design" rendered straight on, NOT concept art.

anti-AI look (follow every point):
1. The object is never perfectly frontal and centred. It is seen at an angle (stated in each request), cut by at least one edge of the frame, with real perspective distortion of the lens. Something partly covers it (a hand, another paper, a shadow, a strap, rain drops).
2. Paper is physical: visible paper fibres, slight waviness, curled corners, fold creases with ink cracked along the folds, a tear, tape or glue marks, dirt and fingerprints, sun-fading on the side that faced the light. Nothing is pristine.
3. Printing is physical: when the camera is close, the printed photographs show halftone dots (offset CMYK rosette) or the coarse dot of cheap newsprint; colours are slightly misregistered (one plate shifted by half a millimetre, leaving a thin coloured fringe); flat spot colours are uneven where the ink was thin; black ink is a deep grey, never pure digital black. No gradients, no glows, no lens flares, no 3D or metallic lettering, no drop shadows printed on the paper: only what a real press of the year 2100 could put on paper, which is flat ink.
4. Typography: only the exact words given in quotes in each request may be readable, spelled exactly as written, in the typeface style described (real-world classes: condensed grotesque, didone serif, slab serif, monospaced, stencil). Letters have even, correct spacing and a consistent typeface. ALL other text (columns of body copy, small print, captions, prices) is either out of focus from the depth of field, or too small to resolve, or hidden; never invented gibberish words in focus.
5. The printed photographs ON the object look like real press photographs of the year 2100: natural light, real people with pores and asymmetric faces, slightly unflattering moments, a bit of motion blur; never glossy portraits with glowing rim light. The design layout has the restraint of real editorial design: a grid, two or three ink colours, lots of plain paper.
6. The light on the scene is the real ambient light of the place (stated in each request): no added cinematic glow, no teal-and-orange grade, no vignette, no HDR. Exposure is honest: a window can blow out to white, a corner can go black.
7. The camera is real: stated lens and aperture with genuine depth of field (the background is truly soft), mild chromatic aberration at the edges, fine grain or sensor noise matching the ISO, a slight tilt of the horizon from a handheld shot.
8. The surroundings are ordinary and specific, full of small, unglamorous objects. Nobody poses, nobody looks into the lens.
9. No real brands, no real logos, no real flags, no real political symbols, no watermark, no signature.

title: "Kiosco en la estación Fulton" - a news kiosk in a New York subway station in 2100: the cover of the business weekly everyone reads in the Corporate Union, with President Valeria Castellane.

Its president, Valeria Castellane, comes from the water trust that controls the desalination plants; she is in her second four-year term and every quarterly report is a small panic. In this world its share price is followed like a national mood. Palette of the Union: navy blue, silver, white, small gold.

camera:
- 35mm lens, f/2.8, 1/60 s, ISO 1600. Handheld, standing in front of the kiosk at chest height, angled about 30 degrees from the left, so the magazines recede in perspective to the right.
- Focus on the one magazine at left-centre; everything else falls off softly.

composition:
- The kiosk: a narrow steel news stand of the year 2100 wedged between tiled subway columns, its racks packed with printed magazines and folded newspapers in overlapping rows, clipped with wooden clothes pegs, a little hand-written price card (out of focus), a small transparent rain cover pushed back. Printed paper still sells because it cannot be edited after the fact.
- Hero magazine, left-centre, in a wire rack, slightly bent forward under its own weight, the top right corner curled, a thin orange price sticker on it (unreadable):
  - Masthead across the top, a heavy condensed grotesque in white on a navy band: "TICKER".
  - Cover photo: Valeria Castellane, a woman of 51 with long wavy dark-brown hair with lighter streaks, strong brows, almond eyes, defined cheekbones, red lipstick, a faint scar under the chin, small wrinkles at the eyes, navy suit, photographed from slightly below at a press conference, caught mid-blink with her mouth half-open, a microphone foam edge entering the frame; real press-photo halftone visible up close.

- The kiosk owner's hand, old, with a wedding ring and a band-aid on one knuckle, enters from the top right edge adjusting a newspaper, cut by the frame.
- Bottom 15% (calm): the kiosk's scuffed steel counter, a few coins, a folded paper, in shadow.
